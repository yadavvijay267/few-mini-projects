Chapter 1

The Spark

It starts in a laser no bigger than a grain of rice. A pulse of infrared light — invisible to the eye, faster than anything else humans have built — is born in a transmitter rack in Noida.

One pulse. One bit. Then another, and another — 0 pulses every second.

Chapter 2

The Glass

The pulse enters a fiber thinner than a human hair. Its core is pure silica glass, so clear that if the ocean were made of it, you could see the seabed from the surface.

Total internal reflection bounces the light along the core — 0 km per second, two-thirds the speed of light in a vacuum.

Chapter 3 · The Route

Ducts under the road. Poles along the highway. The cable is pulled, metre by metre, through a city that never stops moving.

40 km of duct

Every joint is logged, every closure photographed, every splice tested before the next span begins.

0.2 dB per km

That is all the light the fiber loses. After 40 km, the pulse is still strong enough to read.

Chapter 4

The Splice

Two fiber ends meet in a fusion splicer. An electric arc melts the glass; the cores align to within a micron. The joint is tested with an OTDR — a radar for light.

A good splice loses 0 hundredths of a decibel. A bad one is cut out and done again. Quality control is the whole job.

Chapter 5

The Light

Forty kilometres later, the pulse arrives. A photodiode turns it back into electricity, and a video call, a bank transfer, a homework upload — someone's ordinary moment — happens because the light made it.

That is the story of fiber: invisible infrastructure, carrying visible lives.
